music-score features

A small vanilla JavaScript library that draws music notation and guitar tablature as SVG, from a plain JSON score, using the Bravura (SMuFL) music font. No build step, no dependencies.

Every example on this page is rendered live by the library. Expand "Score JSON" under an example to see its input. Full field reference: README.md.

This page loads ES modules and a font file, so it must be served over http. From the project folder run python -m http.server 8765 --bind 127.0.0.1 and open http://127.0.0.1:8765/docs/features.html.

Quick start

import { MusicScoreRenderer, defaultTheme } from './index.js';

const renderer = new MusicScoreRenderer(document.getElementById('score'), score, { width: 860 });
renderer.render();          // draws (and redraws) the whole SVG; returns the renderer
renderer.setScore(other);   // swap the score, then call render() again

Notation and tablature

A track can show a notation staff, a tab staff, or both. Notes given by string and fret get their pitch from the track's tuning and are written an octave above sounding pitch, as guitar music is, under an 8vb clef. Six- and four-string tabs use the SMuFL TAB clef. A tab-only track shows its rhythm below the tab staff (stems, beams, flags, dots, tuplets, with rests inside the staff); set tabRhythm: false to hide it, or tabRhythm: true to add it to a tab staff under notation.

Multi-instrument systems

Tracks stack into systems. Beats that start at the same time line up on every staff. Staves are joined by a line; a notation + tab pair is bracketed when the system holds two or more instruments. The full name is drawn on the first system and the short label after that. Bass parts can use writtenOctaveUp to be written an octave above sounding pitch.

Page header

title is centred, composer is right-aligned, tuningLabel sits at the left under the title, and tempoText sits above the first system at the time signature.

Line breaks and justification

  • Measures are spaced by duration and broken into systems to fit the width. Every system except a short last one is stretched to full width.
  • The time signature appears only at the start and where it changes; clef and key repeat on each system.
  • A key or time change at a line break gets a courtesy signature at the end of the previous system.
  • Measure numbers mark the start of systems 2 onward.
  • Vertical gaps follow the content, so tall passages get more room.

Barlines and repeats

Barline types: single, double, final, repeatStart, repeatEnd. Barlines are drawn per staff, so they never run through the gap between notation and tab. A barline just before a repeat start merges into the repeat sign, and an end repeat followed by a start repeat becomes one combined sign.

Endings and navigation

Measure fields for volta brackets and navigation marks. They apply to the whole measure column, so you can set them on any track; on a conflict the lowest track wins. They are drawn above the top staff of each system and do not change the horizontal spacing.

FieldDrawn as
endingVolta bracket labelled "1." or "1., 2.": 1, [1, 2], or { numbers, text, open } (text replaces the label, open overrides the closing hook)
segno: trueSegno sign at the start of the measure
coda: trueCoda sign at the start of the measure
jumpBold italic text ending at the closing barline: D.C., D.C. al Fine, D.C. al Coda, D.S., D.S. al Fine, D.S. al Coda, To Coda (with a small coda sign), Fine; other text is drawn as written
  • Consecutive measures with the same ending share one bracket. A different ending, a measure without one, or a repeat start begins a new one.
  • A bracket gets a closing hook when its last measure ends with an end repeat; otherwise it stays open.
  • A bracket that crosses a line break continues on the next system without a number.
  • Brackets sit above chord symbols, chord diagrams and fermatas, and the signs and jump texts sit above the brackets. The tempo stays on top.
  • Nothing is played or unrolled: the marks are drawn as written.

Rhythm and beaming

Durations from whole to 64th, dots, rests and tuplets. Eighths and shorter beam within each beat; compound meters such as 6/8 and 12/8 beam by dotted beat. An empty measure draws a centred whole-measure rest.

Pitch, keys and accidentals

Key signatures from 7 flats to 7 sharps, changeable mid-score. Accidentals follow standard rules: they last to the end of the measure, a cancelling natural is added when needed, and stacked accidentals in chords are spread into columns. Notes a second apart in a chord sit on either side of the stem.

Ties and slurs

Ties connect notes of the same pitch (enharmonic spellings count), slurs span phrases. Both sit opposite the stems, and both are split into two arcs when they cross a line break.

Articulations, fermatas, dynamics, chord symbols

Staccato, tenuto and accent go on the side opposite the stem and stack in a fixed order. Fermatas sit above everything on the beat. Chord symbols share one line above each system and dynamics one line below, both kept clear of the notes.

Chord diagrams

Fretboard boxes above the chord symbols, as in lead sheets and guitar books. List the shapes once in the score's chordShapes, keyed by chord name; every beat whose chord matches gets a diagram with the name centred above it.

FieldMeaning
fretsOne entry per string, low to high like tuning: a fret from 0 to 24, or 'x' / null for a muted string
fingersOptional, low to high: 1 to 4 or 'T', drawn under the grid
barreOptional { fret, from, to }. Strings are numbered like notes (1 = highest); the default runs from the lowest string to string 1
baseFretOptional first fret of the grid. By default the grid starts at the nut, or at the lowest fret with "5fr" beside it when a fret is above 4
  • Tracks with TAB show diagrams by default. A notation-only lead sheet opts in with showChordDiagrams: true; false turns them off for a track.
  • A beat's chordDiagram replaces the dictionary shape for that beat, and chordDiagram: false keeps a plain chord symbol.
  • An invalid shape (wrong number of strings, a bad fret, more than 5 frets apart) falls back to the plain chord symbol.
  • The diagrams of a system's staff sit in one row above the music, and their names share one line with the plain chord symbols. Fermatas, endings, signs and the tempo sit above them.
  • On a tab-only track the diagrams go above the TAB, outside pick strokes, labels and bends. Diagrams take no horizontal space, so close chord changes can overlap.

Harmonics

Set harmonic on a note. Natural harmonics (true or 'natural') get a diamond notehead and a <12> fret on tab, with no label. Artificial, tapped, pinch and semi harmonics keep a normal notehead and get an A.H., T.H., P.H. or S.H. label above the tab. harmonicFret adds the touched fret, as in 5(17).

  • Consecutive beats with the same label share one dashed line, as palm mute does. P.M. sits nearest the staff, and a harmonic label sits outside it.
  • Labels move clear of bends. A muted note is drawn as an x, with no harmonic.
  • Grace notes get a diamond or TAB text, but no harmonic label.

Picking and playing directions

Beat fields for the marks players read: letRing for a let-ring run, pickStroke for down and up picks, strum and arpeggio for strummed and spread chords, tap, slap, pop and trill on a note, and whammy and rake on a beat. None of them change the rhythm; only arpeggio adds room left of its chord.

  • Consecutive letRing beats share one dashed "let ring" label, and an unmarked rest between them keeps the run going. The label sits outside any P.M. or harmonic label.
  • Pick strokes sit above the notation staff, one per beat, and beats in one beam group share a height. On a tab-only track they sit above the TAB.
  • A tapped note gets a "T" above its note or fret. A tapped harmonic shows only its T.H. label.
  • A whammy dip is drawn on the TAB staff only, as a V with its amount above it. true means 1/2.
  • A whammy dive slopes down (0.8 to 2.4 sp deep, by amount) and stays held, as a level line, until a beat with { type: 'return' }, where it rises again. A dip, another dive or a pre-dive ends the hold early with no rise. A hold carries over a line break, stopping 0.5 sp before the barline and resuming after the next header without text. A pre-dive is a short level lead-in before its note, then the rise. The line is lifted above any frets, marks, letters, trills and bends under it.
  • A slapped or popped bass note gets an "S" or "P" above it, in the same place as the "T". One letter per beat: tap, then slap, then pop.
  • A rake is a dashed "rake" label over its beat and the grace notes right before it. Write those dead-note grace notes yourself. The label sits outside any P.M. or let-ring label it overlaps.
  • A trill is "tr" and a wavy line above the beat, on notation and TAB, above any vibrato or tremolo and below a "T", pick stroke or fermata. On TAB, a fret or pitch target adds (7). Consecutive trills on a system share one height. The wave stops before the next beat (or the next "tr") and at a line break.
  • A strum arrow ('down' ↓, 'up' ↑) sits above the TAB staff, clear of the frets and marks under it, or above the notation staff on a track without TAB. Consecutive strums on a system share one height, so a rasgueado run reads as one line. A strum replaces the beat's pick stroke.
  • An arpeggio is a wavy line just left of a chord, on notation and TAB, spanning its notes. 'up' adds an arrowhead at the top, 'down' at the bottom. The chord gets 1 sp more room on its left, and grace notes stay left of the line.
  • Rests and grace notes ignore pick strokes, strums, arpeggios, taps, slaps, pops, trills, whammy marks and rakes. A single note ignores an arpeggio.

Rhythm on tab

A tab-only track (showStandardNotation: false, showTablature: true) shows its rhythm under the TAB staff by default, as in tab books: stems, beams, flags, augmentation dots, tuplets and rests. Set tabRhythm: false to keep plain fret numbers. A track that also shows notation gets this only with tabRhythm: true.

  • Stems hang below the strings, one per note or chord. Half notes get a short stem, and whole notes none.
  • Eighths and shorter beam as in notation. Unbeamed eighths to 64ths get smaller flags.
  • A tuplet shows its number under a single beam, or a bracket otherwise.
  • Rests sit inside the staff. An empty measure (beats: []) shows a whole-measure rest.
  • The band adds height below the TAB staff. Marks above the staff keep their place.

Theming

Pass options.theme to override any key of defaultTheme: colours (noteColor, staffLineColor, tabFretColor, background), fonts, line widths and spacing. Spacing keys ending in Sp are in staff spaces, so staffLineSpacing scales the whole score. Rhythm on tab uses tabStemGapSp, tabStemLengthSp, tabHalfStemLengthSp, tabFlagScale and tabFretColor.

Not included

  • Playback, an editor, and import/export of other formats (design notes for these are in ARCHITECTURE.md).
  • Multiple voices in one track, and different time signatures across tracks in the same measure.
  • Glyph and text widths are estimated, not measured from the font.
